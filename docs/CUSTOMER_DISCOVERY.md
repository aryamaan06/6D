# 6D: first customer-discovery brief

Prepared October 10, 2026. This is a hypothesis, not validated demand, pricing or traction. No outreach has been sent.

## The first problem to test

A mission-analysis engineer changes code, assumptions or settings and must establish whether the new numerical result is trustworthy enough for a design review. An audit packet containing source versions, declared assumptions, independently computed comparisons and explicit exclusions may make that review easier.

The first implementation demonstrates a verifier component using synthetic circular orbits. It does not yet accept a customer's model, cover an operational mission, or establish a commercial advantage.

## Proposed early buyers and users

- Technical leads at small spacecraft developers and specialist mission-analysis consultancies.
- Engineers who own numerical-model changes and reviewers who must examine their evidence.
- Laboratories can provide technical feedback; this is not evidence of enterprise buying intent.

Floating Coliseum is motivation, not a substitute for external customer validation.

## Questions for a future interview

Ask about a recent actual change, not whether the person likes the 6D idea:

1. Walk us through the last change to a mission-analysis model. What changed and who reviewed it?
2. What checks did you run? What was hard to reproduce or inspect later?
3. Describe a numerical result that ran successfully but needed investigation. How was it found?
4. Which exact tools, models and file formats are involved? What data can leave your environment?
5. How long did evidence preparation and review take? Which part was repeated?
6. What do existing tools already do well, including requirements systems and simulation workflows?
7. Who would authorize a pilot, what evidence would they require, and what budget might apply?
8. Could we evaluate one low-risk, non-sensitive workflow using agreed reference cases and a documented baseline?

Do not ask for confidential flight software or customer data casually. No messages, meetings or commitments are authorized by this document.

## Pilot proposal to validate later

One supported numerical workflow, one representative model change, agreed checks and exclusions. Compare with the customer's current practice and incumbent tools. Record setup effort, review effort, unsupported findings, known defects missed, false accusations, reproducibility and subsequent reuse. A technical reviewer must assess check applicability. Report all outcomes, including losses.

Discuss pricing only after learning the scope, integration/support burden and budget owner. The earlier investor-deck pricing was illustrative and has not been adopted as a commercial price.

## Competitive question

Flow already describes connected requirements, systems and tests, change impact, AI assistance and immutable history. Its existence means generic evidence tracking is not a demonstrated differentiator. Our narrower hypothesis is useful, independently executed numerical investigations with a low setup burden. We must compare that workflow rather than claim competitors cannot provide it.

Primary source: https://www.flowengineering.com/product/requirements-management (checked October 10, 2026).

## What earns the next investment of time

- Relevant engineers describe a repeated problem using concrete recent examples.
- A bounded customer workflow can be supported with credible checks and affordable setup.
- Independent review finds the evidence useful and appropriately limited.
- A buyer agrees to a defined pilot or another meaningful commitment; compliments are not payment evidence.

If the need is already adequately solved, or setup/support remains bespoke, revise the first entry before expanding into more engineering disciplines.
